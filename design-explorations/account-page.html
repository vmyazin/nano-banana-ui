The /account console — four ways to use the rail

Round one picked the console direction: a settings rail beside a library canvas. All four variants below are that same frame with the same account record — what changes is what the rail is, and what that leaves the canvas free to do. Round-one directions A, C and D (capacity control room, readiness checklist, ledger) have been cleared.

What works

Zero navigation: the quota you are about to blow and the key you need to add are both on screen while you browse. Folding the in-flight strip and the temporary banner into Needs attention and Temporary pills buys back roughly 130px above the fold — the grid now starts under the title — and the two states that cost money or expire are still one tap away, without the routing B needs. With the connection form gone the rail settles into one shape per group — a summary and a single button — so the rarest action no longer takes the most space.

What risks

It does not scale — an eighth connection or a second storage tier pushes deletion off-screen and the rail becomes its own scroll region. Everything is equally loud, so nothing reads as primary. And a counted pill is a weaker signal than a banner: a job that needs a person now looks the same as a media-type filter, with no prompt text to recognise it by.

Pick this if

You want the smallest change from what is built today, and accounts realistically stay small.

B

Rail as index

Optimizes for giving every panel the full canvas when it is the thing you came for — the rail becomes a counted navigation list, not a column of forms. · Tradeoff settings cost a click, and the quota has to be duplicated under the nav because it is the one number that must stay on screen while you browse.

What works

The counts do real work — "Jobs 3 · 1 !" tells you there is something to fix without spending vertical space on it, and connections finally get a full-width screen instead of a 260px form. A narrower rail buys the grid ~10px per card.

What risks

It invents routing this page does not have — five destinations, each needing a URL, a back behaviour and an empty state. The in-flight strip disappears from the library view, so a running job is only a number until you navigate to it.

Pick this if

You expect the account area to keep growing — billing, teams, more providers — and want somewhere to put the next panel that is not "further down the rail".

C

Inspector — rail flipped right, cards go quiet

Optimizes for acting on one result: selecting a card fills a right-hand inspector with the full prompt, model, input mode and control values, and every action moves there — so the cards themselves lose their buttons and get cleaner at 4-up. · Tradeoff account settings become the inspector's idle state, which demotes them, and every action now costs a select-then-act instead of one click.

Account settings

What works

It surfaces data the product already stores and currently throws away — metadata.modelId, inputMode, referenceIds and the whole values record are on every asset and shown nowhere today. It also fixes the 4-up squeeze honestly: the cards have no buttons to cram in.

What risks

Settings end up behind a button in the corner of a media browser, which is a strange home for account deletion. Selection needs a real empty state, and on tablet the inspector has to become a sheet — the biggest build cost of the four.

Pick this if

The library is where people spend their time and "how did I make this?" is a question worth answering — this is the only variant that can answer it.

D

Compact rail + capacity band

Optimizes for keeping the spine while giving the quota and in-flight jobs the full width they had in round one's control room — the rail shrinks to 220px of summaries. · Tradeoff two horizontal bands stand between you and the first thumbnail, and the storage figure is stated twice on one screen.

What works

Recovers the one thing round one's control room got right — you cannot miss the quota or a job needing attention — without giving up the spine. The 220px rail also buys the widest cards of the four at ~208px.

What risks

Redundant by construction: storage appears in the band and the rail has to stay quiet about it, which makes the rail feel arbitrary. Title, band and warning stack up before any work is visible.

Pick this if

Storage overflow is a live support problem and you are unwilling to trade the spine away to fix it.

How to pick a direction

Same console frame, same account record, same six panels. Choose on what the rail is for.

Pick A if →The rail is a settings drawer. Smallest build, nothing hidden, stops scaling at about eight connections.
Pick B if →The rail is navigation. Each panel gets the full canvas, and there is room for the next one you add.
Pick C if →The rail is an inspector. Cards go quiet, and the model, input mode and control values behind each result finally show up.
Pick D if →The rail is a summary and the band carries the urgent numbers. Quota is unmissable; the screen repeats itself.

Cheapest real upgrade: A's rail with C's inspector behaviour — keep the settings spine, and let a selected card open its detail in place of the connection form. It needs no routing and no new empty states.
