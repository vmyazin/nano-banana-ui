Timeline editor — four layout directions

Four ways to lay out the timeline workspace so the track is never below the fold, drawn for editors who already know CapCut and DaVinci Resolve. Every preview is rendered at the ratio where the problem bites: a short landscape window, 1280 × 560.

The problem being solved

Today the workspace is a document that scrolls: a header card, then a two-column grid whose right column stacks preview → output bar → export → track. The preview sizes itself to the export frame and is only capped by a max-height, so on a short landscape window the 16:9 frame plus two cards consume the whole viewport and the track — the thing being edited — starts below the fold.

Every direction below keeps the same features and the same copy. What changes is where the vertical budget goes and who owns the scroll.

A

Fixed NLE shell

Optimizes for the track being structurally impossible to lose — one 100dvh frame, no page scroll, pool · viewer · inspector over a docked timeline with a drag splitter. · Tradeoff gives up the app's document behaviour, and needs an honest fallback below roughly 640px of height where three bands stop fitting.

What works

The fold problem stops being a layout accident and becomes impossible: the viewer is a flex child that shrinks, so any viewport shape spends its leftover height on the frame and keeps the track at a fixed floor. It is also the shape a Resolve or CapCut user reaches for without reading anything — pool left, viewer centre, deliver right, timeline bottom.

What risks

It is the largest change: the workspace stops being a card stack inside the app's scrolling page and becomes its own shell, which affects the top nav, the account chrome and every narrow-width fallback. Below ~640px of height, three bands plus the app bar genuinely do not fit and something has to collapse.

Pick this if

The timeline is meant to feel like a real editor rather than a page in this app, and you are willing to give it a dedicated route with its own chrome.

B

Adaptive pane + sticky dock

Optimizes for the smallest change that fixes the bug — the page still scrolls, but the preview is capped by viewport height rather than a constant, and the track is a sticky bottom dock that can never scroll away. · Tradeoff the dock overlays the document, so the middle band is permanently shorter and long content passes underneath a floating element.

What works

It is reachable in one pass: the grid, the cards and the copy all survive, and two changes carry it — a viewport-derived cap on the preview frame and position: sticky on the track. The grip doubles as the answer to "I want more timeline", so the dock can grow without a second layout.

What risks

A sticky dock is a floating element over a scrolling document, so content genuinely passes beneath it and the reading area shrinks by the dock's height at every scroll position. The editor still reads as a web page rather than a tool, which is the opposite of the familiarity goal.

Pick this if

You want the fold bug gone this week without re-founding the workspace, and the CapCut resemblance can arrive later on top of it.

C

Timeline-first edit desk

Optimizes for the editing surface itself — the track takes most of the window with a video lane and an audio lane, the pool collapses to an icon rail, and the viewer demotes to a floating program monitor that can be collapsed away. · Tradeoff a small monitor is a bad place to judge framing, which is exactly what the per-clip contain/cover control asks you to do.

What works

The window is spent on the thing being edited, and the lane structure is the honest place to put the audio decision — "Keep audio" stops being a checkbox in a settings strip and becomes a track you can see. Collapsing the monitor is a real gesture editors use when they are cutting rather than reviewing.

What risks

The audio lane is drawn from data the product does not have: nothing decodes waveforms, and per-clip gain/mute are still marked as future work in the store. Shipping the lane empty would promise an editing model that is not there, and the shrunken monitor undercuts the fit and frame-ratio controls the preview exists to justify.

Pick this if

Per-clip audio and multi-lane editing are actually next on the roadmap, and this layout is meant to make room for them rather than only to fix the fold.

D

Tabbed workbench with clip inspector

Optimizes for vertical budget by collapsing four stacked cards into one tabbed panel — Media / Format / Export / Clip — beside a large viewer, with a transport bar carrying timecode and key hints and the timeline permanently docked. · Tradeoff it needs a clip-selection model the codebase does not have yet, and the controls that are always visible today move behind tabs.

What works

Four stacked cards became one panel, which is where the vertical budget was actually going, and the viewer gets the space back. The inspector is the natural home for trim numbers that are currently only reachable by dragging a handle, and the key hints teach the transport that already exists — Space, and Alt+arrow reorder.

What risks

The Clip tab is empty until a selection model exists — clips are focusable today, not selectable — and volume and speed are not in the data at all. Tabs also hide the output format, which currently sits in the open and is the thing "auto" quietly changes as clips arrive.

Pick this if

You want the Resolve page model — one context panel, one docked timeline — and are ready to add clip selection as the foundation it needs.

How to pick a direction

All four remove the fold bug. They differ in how much of the editor they re-found to do it.

If you want →A real editor shell where the track can never be lost, and a dedicated route is acceptable — pick A.
If you want →The bug fixed now, with the current cards and copy intact — pick B.
If you want →Room for per-clip audio and lanes, and the cut to outrank the review — pick C.
If you want →Resolve's one-panel-at-a-time density, and clip selection on the roadmap — pick D.
Hybrid worth namingA's shell with D's tabbed panel is the most CatCut/Resolve-like end state; B is the honest first step toward it.
