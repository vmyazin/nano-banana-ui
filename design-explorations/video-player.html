Instrument rail

What works

A 2px track with one cyan-glow thumb reads as measurement rather than media playback, and the mono readout matches the eyebrow type already in the app. Because the bar sits below the frame it never covers the result — the thing the viewer actually came to look at.

What risks

The 2px track is the smallest pointer target here, and the compact rendering drops to 8px of thumb. It also adds roughly 34px of vertical chrome to every gallery cell, which multiplies down a grid.

Pick this if

the clip is the subject and the controls should stay out of the picture entirely.

B

Floating scrim

Optimizes for one identical treatment at both densities, and the largest touch targets · Tradeoff permanently covers the bottom fifth of the frame, so the last band of every result is read through a gradient

What works

The most conventional reading of a video player, so nobody has to learn it — and the only variant whose compact and full bars are structurally the same object, which is exactly the “recognisably the same instrument” goal. A 14px thumb is comfortable on touch.

What risks

It sits on top of the image. For a generation tool that is a real cost: the bottom of a 9:16 portrait clip is content someone is judging, and a scrim over it changes what they see. Reveal-on-hover fixes that at the price of the cross-density consistency that is the variant’s whole argument.

Pick this if

conventional beats bespoke, and you would rather nobody think about the player at all.

C

Minimal seam

Optimizes for the least chrome possible — a resting cell shows a 3px line and nothing else · Tradeoff hides every control but the scrubber until hover, so the player is nearly invisible on touch

What works

At rest a gallery of twelve clips is twelve clean frames with a thin cyan progress line, which is the quietest a grid can be while still showing progress. The centred round play button on hover is a strong, obvious affordance.

What risks

It leans hardest on hover, and the spec requires the compact bar pinned visible on coarse pointers — which on touch collapses this into something close to B, so the two read differently on desktop and nearly identically on a phone. Volume and fullscreen have nowhere to live in this layout yet.

Pick this if

the gallery is the primary surface and its calm at rest matters more than discoverability.

D

Console

Optimizes for explicitness — every control present, labelled, and comfortably sized at full density · Tradeoff the heaviest chrome, and the recessed frame makes the result look smaller than it is

Variant D · console
Full density · result panel · “arcade dolly 4s” · fal

Compact · 180px cell · “skylight rain push”

What works

The only variant that shows a real volume slider rather than a mute toggle, and the elevated shell ties the player to the glass-card surfaces already in the app. A 6px track is easy to hit and reads clearly at a glance.

What risks

It frames the result in furniture. On a page whose entire job is presenting a generated clip, a bezel and a control console compete with the thing being presented — and the compact rendering throws away almost everything the variant argues for, so the two densities read as different components. Measured: its shell padding shrinks the 180px cell’s frame to 158×119, against 180×135 in every other variant — the clip itself comes out smaller.

Pick this if

the result panel is the primary surface and you want the controls to feel like equipment.
